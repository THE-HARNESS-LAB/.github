## What we do

There is a category of professional work that is 90% judgment and 10% typing —
and where the typing eats most of the week anyway. Title examination. Records
review. Anything where the deliverable is a document that somebody's reputation
is attached to.

The Harness Lab builds the software that eats the mechanical part, and leaves
the judgment where it belongs. We do it **on the professional's own machine**,
because the documents in question are client business, and client business does
not belong in somebody else's cloud as the price of being read.

## How we build

**Local first.** The records stay on your computer. Title files contain client
business, mineral ownership, and personal information that has no business being
uploaded to somebody's cloud in order to be read.

**Traceable.** Every figure in a deliverable points back to the page it came
from. If a number can't be traced, it doesn't belong in the report.

**Exact.** Ownership is computed in exact fractions, not floating point. A
decimal that drifts in the sixth place is a decimal that gets argued about in a
title opinion.

**Deliverable-shaped.** The output is the thing that actually gets turned in, in
the format the client asked for — not an export you have to rebuild by hand.

## Who builds it

**Spencer Teague** — founder, and the person who writes the code.

The Harness Lab is deliberately small. The advantage of small is that there is
no gap between the person who understands the problem and the person who can
change the software: a landman describes something the tool gets wrong on a
Tuesday, and it is fixed in the build, not filed in a backlog.

That shows up in the support policy, which is not a policy so much as a fact —
**the person who wrote it answers the email.** No tier-one queue, no ticket
number, no engineer three handoffs away from the person who knows why the code
does what it does.

The products come from watching the actual work: not "how do we put AI in title
research," but *which specific hours of a title project are being spent on
things a person's judgment is wasted on* — and then removing exactly those, with
the output still traceable line by line to the record it came from.
